LensSpark shows what's on your phone on the glasses as a slow slideshow: the newest picture about once a second. It isn't video, but it's great for anything you'd glance at.

What it's good for

Sing along

Spotify with lyrics on screen: the words follow the song.

Read a book

Kindle or any e-reader. Landscape and a dark theme read best.

Maps

Google Maps, Waze and other maps. Be safe: don't let the glasses take your eyes off the road, and follow local laws.

Follow the game

Keep a scoreboard or play-by-play in view.

Browse

Web pages, articles, recipes while your hands are busy.

Your feeds

Keep up on social feeds, news and messages.

Hands-free control

There's no ring control yet; you control what's shown from the phone. Android's Voice Access works really well once you learn it ("scroll down", "tap Next"), and Bixby or Google Assistant can get you a long way.

Install

  1. Download the APK on your phone and open it. If Android asks, allow your browser (or Discord) to install unknown apps. Play Protect may warn about an unknown developer: choose Install anyway.
  2. Open LensSpark and tap Connect. Allow Nearby devices and notifications.
  3. Tap Share screen and accept Android's screen-sharing prompt.

Good to know

  • Locking the phone stops sharing. Android ends screen capture on lock. Unlock and tap Share screen.
  • High quality is the slowest. Use Fast or Balanced for anything that moves.
  • While sharing, the glasses' display won't stay asleep. Stop sharing to let it sleep.
  • With GATT, INMO Global navigation and your shared screen take turns on the display.
  • Secure screens (banking apps, password fields, most video apps) show as black, as Android does.
  • If the connection drops, LensSpark reconnects by itself, up to 5 tries.

Privacy

No internet permission, no analytics, no accounts. Nothing captured is saved. Your screen goes from your phone to your glasses over Bluetooth and nowhere else.
